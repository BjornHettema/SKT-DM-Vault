## Backstory (as written by the player)
I was once a respected artificer of a powerful clan in north Faerûn. I was tasked with designing a construct meant to protect our people a guardian capable of shielding civilians from rival clans and raiders. I poured my heart into it. It was more than just a mere construct to me, I saw a shield, a warrior, a guardian that would protect our people. It was my masterpiece.

Then my clan uncovered an ancient arcane fragment, a keystone shard pulsing with sealed power. The clan mages deciphered enough to recognize its potential, and the clan leaders declared it a weapon of conquest. With it, they would erase rival clans and dominate the Vast. I was ordered to make that power usable. Reluctantly, I bound the shard to my creation.

The infusion changed it. Arcane energy flowed through stone veins I had forged. It began manifesting abilities no artificer could design powerful arcane blasts, shield manifestation, the power to weaken someone by just will alone. It was beyond my understanding. But it was powerful. So the clan approved.

Before it was fully stabilized, they deployed it in a “test.” I witnessed the first campaign. I watched in horror as buildings were reduced to ash, soldiers and civilians cut down in calculated efficiency. I created the construct to protect people, not for this. But the clan thought it was perfect. I only thought about the ashes it left behind in it's campaign.

I couldn't bear to think about the destruction it would bring, the innocent lives it would take. So I decided I needed to destroy it. To undo what I had allowed. I tried to sever the fragment, the source of its ethereal power. The fragment reacted. The construct reacted. In a surge of unstable arcane force, construct and creator became one. One body. Two consciousnesses. The construct's shell shattered. The shard fractured. My mind followed. The power that once flowed freely now lies dormant, incomplete like a system awaiting reactivation. In that moment I heard another thought that was not my own, it told me to run.

Now our memories come in fragments, flashes of fire, the sound of metal striking stone, a voice that could be ours or perhaps something neither of us knows anything about. When we try too hard to remember the past, something forces us back. Whether there is more behind that fragment than we thought, or whether it is simply the result of a body sharing two minds, we do not know yet. What we do know is that we must not fall back into the hands of the clan. For our lives aren't determined, we will make our own destinies.
