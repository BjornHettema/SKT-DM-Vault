## Backstory (as written by the player)
**B’Leep, the Dreamwright**

B’Leep lived beneath an apple tree in the front garden of an elderly woman’s cottage. His small home was built from brick fragments, copper pipes, discarded shutters, and colored glass. The cottage’s owner knew he was there and occasionally left him tea, though neither formally acknowledged their arrangement.

He lived neither richly nor poorly, earning his keep by repairing tools, locks, clocks, and household machinery. Small and physically weak even for a gnome, B’Leep relied on patience, intelligence, and invention. Where others used strength, he built machines to lift, reach, and carry for him.

Calm and observant, B’Leep is cautious around strangers and judges people by how they treat those who cannot benefit them. He especially despises cruelty and abuse. His anger is rarely loud, but it is precise and slow to fade.


**The Mountain**

B’Leep’s family loved exploring the mountains, collecting unusual stones and sketching old ruins along the way. One morning, after B’Leep had worked through the night, they left without him.

A sudden storm struck the mountain, causing the path to collapse beneath ice and falling stone. None of his family returned.

B’Leep has blamed himself ever since. He constantly imagines inventions that might have saved them: warning devices, stronger climbing equipment, or machines capable of predicting the weather. Their deaths inspired him to create constructs that prevent disaster rather than merely repair its aftermath.


**The Mentor**

Not long after the accident, a traveling artificer noticed B’Leep’s work.

The artificer presented himself as a mentor and praised B’Leep as a rare prodigy. He brought exotic materials to the garden workshop, introduced B’Leep to advanced enchantment techniques, and encouraged him to attempt increasingly complicated creations.

For a time, B’Leep believed he had found someone who truly understood him.

Slowly, however, the lessons became demands. The artificer claimed B’Leep’s designs as his own and sold them to wealthy collectors. He withheld knowledge to keep B’Leep dependent on him, isolated him from other craftspeople, and dismissed every objection as ingratitude. Whenever an experiment succeeded, the mentor took the credit. Whenever one failed, B’Leep carried the blame.

B’Leep endured the mistreatment longer than he likes to admit. He told himself that knowledge was worth the humiliation. He told himself that becoming more powerful would allow him to protect others. Most of all, he feared that without the mentor’s instruction, he would never understand the strange source of his abilities.

Then B’Leep created the spinning top.


**The Dreaming Top**

B’Leep often experienced lucid dreams, though he told no one about them. Within those dreams, he could construct impossible mechanisms. He worked in a boundless workshop where gravity could be redirected with a copper valve, moonlight could be wound around a spool, and memories could be hammered into shape.

Most dream-made creations disappeared when he woke. Their principles remained only as scattered impressions that guided his waking inventions.

One night, however, B’Leep dreamed of a spinning top.

It was a small and elegant construct made from a metal he could not identify. Fine symbols shifted across its surface while it turned. As it spun, the dream workshop became sharper and more stable. Tools remained where he left them. Designs that should have been impossible became clear. With the top nearby, B’Leep could conceive of magical constructs far beyond anything he could create while awake.

When morning came, the top was resting in his open hand.

B’Leep never discovered how he had brought it out of the dream. He only knew that it transformed his work. When spun beside an unfinished device, it allowed him to glimpse more advanced versions of the construct, revealing hidden connections between magic and machinery. It did not make creation effortless, but it showed him what might be possible.

His mentor immediately understood its value.

The artificer attempted to take the top, claiming it as payment for B’Leep’s education. For the first time, B’Leep openly refused him. The confrontation that followed destroyed much of the garden workshop. By its end, the mentor had vanished, and the spinning top was gone with him.

B’Leep does not know whether the artificer stole it, sold it, hid it, or lost it while escaping. He only knows that the mentor took more than a treasured possession. He took the one creation that proved B’Leep’s dreams were real.


**The Adventurer**

B’Leep left the apple tree shortly afterward.

He became an adventurer because a workshop offered only controlled problems. The wider world was unpredictable. Ruins, monsters, hostile magic, and desperate journeys forced him to adapt his artificer abilities to situations no teacher could reproduce. Every danger became a test. Every unusual material became a possible component. Every magical artifact offered a new principle to study.

He tells others that he is searching for the spinning top because it is a uniquely powerful construct. That is true, but incomplete.

B’Leep is also searching for his former mentor.

Part of him wants justice. A darker part wants revenge. He imagines confronting the artificer with creations so advanced that his former mentor can no longer dismiss, exploit, or control him. B’Leep fears that recovering the top may not be enough. He wants the person who hurt him to understand that he has become greater without them.

That desire troubles him more than he admits.


**His New Family**

Though cautious around strangers, B’Leep becomes fiercely loyal to those who earn his trust. His adventuring party is now the most important thing in his life.

He shows his affection through preparation rather than words. He repairs their equipment, studies weaknesses in their armor, and builds devices to protect them from danger.

B’Leep could survive losing the spinning top, but he is not sure he could survive losing another family. If a companion were seriously injured or killed, he would see it as proof that he was still not powerful enough to protect those he loves. This fear drives him toward greater inventions, but may also convince him that power is the answer to every possible loss.


**The Secret of the Dreamwright**

B’Leep has told no one the full truth about his talents.

His creations are not solely the result of study. Each night, he enters his lucid workshop and experiments with mechanisms that cannot exist in the waking world. He may spend what feels like weeks refining a construct, only to awaken a few hours later with a half-remembered design scratched into his thoughts.

He hides this because he does not understand it. He does not know whether the dream workshop comes from his own mind, another plane, or an outside intelligence quietly teaching him. Most frighteningly, he does not know whether he created the spinning top in his dream or whether something within the dream wanted him to carry it into the waking world.

Recently, the workshop has begun to change.

Tools have moved when B’Leep was not looking. Half-finished constructs have appeared on benches he does not remember using. Sometimes, just before waking, he hears a spinning top turning somewhere beyond the workshop walls.
